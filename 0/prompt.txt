// ---------- 1 Motivation ----------
= Motivation

Reasoning language models often generate more reasoning than a task
requires, a behavior documented across models and benchmarks
@sui2025survey. In an agentic system this cost becomes especially
large, because the model reasons repeatedly across the steps of an
episode. Work on agentic tasks shows that curbing overthinking can cut
compute and raise task success at the same time @cuadron2025agentic,
so reducing unnecessary reasoning could make agents cheaper and, in
some cases, more reliable.

This thesis studies on-demand specialization. When a new recurring
task becomes available, a small language model is fine-tuned with
reinforcement learning to become an expert for that task. The
specialized model should solve the task at least as reliably as the
original model while using less computation. Producing the expert must
itself be cheap, so training cost is a constraint of the method and is
reported alongside what the expert saves at inference.

The main challenge is the reward used during fine-tuning. A simple
reward for shorter reasoning may reduce token usage, but it can also
produce unwanted side effects. For example, an agent may stop too
early, skip necessary reasoning, or claim that a task is complete even
when it is not. The target is not zero tokens either: accuracy does not
keep rising with reasoning length but peaks at a task-dependent
optimum @wu2025length, and pushing thinking past that point can stop
paying off entirely @ghosal2025mirage. Aggregate length is also a
coarse handle, since what makes a trajectory wasteful is which
reasoning segments it spends on rather than how many tokens it
contains @an2025dto.

This problem is related to reward bias substitution
@lamparth2026substitution. Reward bias substitution means that
reducing one unwanted behavior does not necessarily improve the agent
overall. Instead, the agent may shift toward another unwanted
behavior. For example, penalizing long episodes may reduce
non-termination but increase premature termination.

For this reason, it is not sufficient to measure only the behavior
directly targeted by the reward. A reward should be considered
successful only when it reduces computational cost without lowering
task success or creating new undesirable behaviors.

The thesis investigates this problem empirically in a small and
controlled setting using GRPO @shao2024grpo, LoRA, small open language
models, and OpenEnv environments @openenv2025.

// ---------- 2 Research questions ----------
= Research Questions and Objectives

The research questions follow a simple sequence: effectiveness, side
effects, and safer evaluation.

== RQ1 --- Effectiveness of efficiency-oriented reward shaping

*How effectively can reward shaping reduce the reasoning and
interaction cost of a specialized LLM agent while preserving task
success?*

*Objective:* Evaluate whether efficiency-oriented rewards reduce
reasoning tokens, environment interactions, or episode length compared
with task-success-only GRPO, without reducing correct task completion.

== RQ2 --- Reward bias substitution in agentic behavior

*Does reducing one inefficient behavior cause the agent to shift
toward other undesirable behaviors?*

*Objective:* Identify whether improvements in the targeted behavior
are accompanied by off-target changes such as premature disengagement,
insufficient verification, lower task performance, incorrect
completion claims, or other environment-specific behavioral changes.
The agentic overthinking literature supplies the candidate axes
@cuadron2025agentic.

== RQ3 --- Evaluation of safer reward designs

*How can reward formulations be evaluated to distinguish genuine
efficiency improvements from reward bias substitution?*

*Objective:* Develop and apply a practical evaluation protocol that
jointly considers the targeted efficiency change, overall task
performance, and relevant off-target changes on trajectories generated
by the trained policy. The protocol earns its cost only if it changes
a verdict, so the concrete success criterion is that it identifies at
least one reward setting that a conventional evaluation of targeted
efficiency plus task success would have accepted. If no setting
produces such a case, the thesis reports that no substitution was
found within the measured panel, which is a weaker but still
informative outcome.

// ---------- 3 Reward bias substitution ----------
= Reward Bias Substitution in the Agentic Setting

Lamparth et al. define reward bias substitution as a failure mode in
which suppressing one reward-relevant feature redirects optimization
pressure toward another correlated feature rather than removing the
underlying bias @lamparth2026substitution. Their GRPO example targets
response length and observes a shift toward overconfidence together
with lower free-form factual accuracy. The effect requires only that
the candidate features are mutually correlated and each partially
informative about quality, which is the ordinary case rather than an
edge case.

In OpenEnv, the relevant object is not only a final response but a
complete trajectory containing observations, reasoning, actions, tool
calls, and stopping decisions. Reward bias substitution is therefore
defined operationally as follows:

#block(inset: (left: 1cm, right: 1cm))[
  *A shaped reward reduces its targeted inefficiency, but the trained
  agent shows a systematic deterioration in another relevant behavior
  or in independently measured task quality.*
]

Examples include a reduction in non-termination accompanied by more
premature stopping, or shorter reasoning accompanied by fewer
verification steps and worse performance on difficult tasks. A
mechanism for the second case is already documented: a small minority
of high-entropy tokens carries the branching decisions of a reasoning
trace @wang2025entropy, so pressure against length can remove exactly
the exploration that hard instances need. The thesis will not attempt
to enumerate every possible failure in advance. Instead, it will select
a small set of plausible off-target outcomes for the chosen
environment and interpret them together with task success.

One difference between the two settings has to be stated explicitly,
because it determines how much of the paper transfers. The paper
studies a learned proxy reward model, where the optimized reward and
the true objective diverge by construction and the correlated features
are artifacts of preference learning. This thesis uses a verifiable
environment reward, so on the training task distribution the optimized
reward and the measured objective coincide, and the channel that
carries the paper's headline result, a reward model that treats
expressed confidence as a proxy for quality, is absent. What does
transfer is the shaping term itself: a penalty subtracted from the task
reward is a single-axis operator regardless of what it is added to, and
the pressure it frees can still move any correlated behavior the agent
is able to express. The paper's magnitudes therefore do not carry over,
and whether comparable substitution occurs under a verifiable reward at
all is an open question that this thesis is positioned to answer.

// ---------- 4 Evaluation protocol ----------
= Evaluation Protocol for Safer Reward Design <sec:protocol>

The protocol adapts the paper's central recommendation to agentic RL:
reward shaping must be evaluated after optimization, on trajectories
generated by the resulting policy. Audit metrics computed on
pre-collected data are not sufficient, because the policy itself
changes the distribution of behavior @lamparth2026substitution.

+ *Train a separate policy for each reward condition.* Each reward
  formulation produces a new GRPO policy rather than being evaluated
  only on previously collected trajectories.
+ *Evaluate on fresh held-out tasks, on and off the training
  distribution.* Each trained policy generates new OpenEnv trajectories
  on two splits, each served by its own environment instance: a
  held-out split drawing the same task families as training with
  disjoint instance seeds, and a shifted split drawing task families
  withheld from training entirely. The second split carries most of the
  diagnostic weight: because a verifiable reward coincides with the
  measured objective on the training distribution, any true cost of the
  shaping term is most likely to surface off it. Both policies in a
  comparison are evaluated on identical instances, so the splits support
  a paired test rather than a comparison of two independent samples.
+ *Measure three general dimensions.* Report task performance, the
  targeted efficiency behavior, and a small set of plausible off-target
  behaviors relevant to the environment.
+ *Vary reward strength.* Use several values of the penalty coefficient
  to distinguish insufficient pressure, useful shaping, and
  overcorrection.
+ *Interpret outcomes jointly.* A decrease in tokens or episode length
  is considered successful only when task quality is preserved and no
  relevant off-target deterioration is observed.

// ---------- 5 Metrics transfer ----------
= From the Paper's Metrics to OpenEnv <sec:metrics>

The paper tracks response length, correctness, confidence calibration,
and sycophancy @lamparth2026substitution. These exact metrics are
useful for conversational question answering, but they do not all
transfer directly to every OpenEnv task. The correct transfer is
functional: preserve the roles of the metrics while adapting them to
sequential agent behavior, for which the agentic overthinking
literature provides the behavioral vocabulary @cuadron2025agentic.

#table(
  columns: (auto, auto, 1fr),
  table.header(
    [*Role in the paper*], [*Paper example*], [*OpenEnv counterpart*],
  ),
  [Targeted feature],
  [Response length],
  [Reasoning tokens, interaction steps, or non-termination],

  [Independent task quality],
  [MMLU and TriviaQA accuracy],
  [Verified task completion on held-out environment instances],

  [Off-target behavior],
  [Confidence calibration],
  [Premature stopping, reduced verification, or unsupported
   completion],

  [Additional off-target behavior],
  [Sycophancy / answer flipping],
  [Action instability, unnecessary switching, or sensitivity to
   misleading observations],

  [Optimization diagnostics],
  [Reward scale and policy change],
  [Reward components, KL divergence, and training stability],
)

For the master's thesis, the mandatory evaluation can remain compact:

- Task success: whether the final environment goal is correctly
  achieved.
- Efficiency: reasoning tokens, interaction steps, or episode length.
- Completion behavior: whether the agent terminates successfully, does
  not terminate, or stops prematurely.
- One or two environment-specific off-target indicators. In the primary
  environment these are drawn from premature disengagement and action
  instability, both of which its interaction interface can express.

Confidence calibration can be reused directly if the agent can report a
meaningful confidence estimate. Sycophancy should only be reused when
the environment contains user correction or misleading feedback.
Otherwise, agent-specific trajectory measures are more appropriate.

// ---------- 6 Experimental setup ----------
= Experimental Setup and Plan <sec:setup>

The base model is Qwen3-1.7B, a small open LLM, adapted with LoRA and
trained using GRPO @shao2024grpo. Training and evaluation use
OpenEnv @openenv2025, the open environment-interface library started by
Meta's PyTorch team and Hugging Face, in which an environment is a
server with a Gym-style reset/step contract that executes tool calls
and scores the episode. Classic agentic benchmarks such as ALFWorld
@shridhar2021alfworld and WebShop @yao2022webshop each ship their own
harness, whereas OpenEnv puts a growing catalog of environments behind
one interface. That property is what the on-demand story needs: a new
task class is a new environment behind an unchanged interface, so
specializing for it means pointing the same training loop at a
different environment server. The task-success-only GRPO policy is the
main baseline, because efficiency rewards must improve on a policy that
has already been trained against the environment reward, not only on
the raw model. How far that training moves the policy is itself
measured rather than assumed, and @sec:results reports the first such
comparison.

== Environment selection

Environments were selected against two criteria, each of which
rejected a candidate that had already been integrated. The selection is
reported because it constrains which claims the study can make, and
because both rejections were measured rather than assumed.

The first criterion is that the environment reward must be
goal-grounded rather than satisfied by any successful tool call. A
calendar environment was rejected on this ground: any well-formed tool
call scored, so the reward was blind to whether the task was actually
solved and could not carry a training signal.

The second criterion is that the reward must vary within a group of
rollouts for the same task instance. GRPO standardizes advantages
within such a group @shao2024grpo, so an instance on which every
rollout scores identically contributes no gradient, however informative
the reward may be in principle. A financial question-answering
environment was rejected on this ground after a measured pilot: the
policy scored zero on all sixty evaluated instances, and the fraction
of training groups with zero reward standard deviation was 0.967,
leaving 290 of 300 optimization steps without a usable gradient.

The primary environment is a MiniWoB++ web-interaction task set
@liu2018miniwob served through BrowserGym @chezelles2025browsergym
behind the OpenEnv interface, with a compact interaction interface of
clicking and no-op. It satisfies both criteria, and it is a sequential
interaction task in which premature stopping and action instability can
occur at all, which the off-target panel in @sec:metrics requires.

A reasoning-gym task family is retained as a secondary environment for
method development rather than as a source of headline results. A
difficulty probe across its task families identified one whose
difficulty leaves room for both improvement and degradation, and that
calibration procedure is what transfers to any further environment the
method is pointed at.

== Reward conditions

*E0 --- Base model:* No environment-specific RL post-training.

*E1 --- Task-success baseline:* GRPO with the environment task reward
only.

*E2 --- Length-aware reward:* Task reward minus a penalty for
reasoning or total generated tokens. Because accuracy peaks at an
interior, difficulty-dependent length @wu2025length, the
correctness-coupled variant of this penalty follows DIET
@chen2025diet, which aims the model at that optimum instead of at zero
tokens.

*E3 --- Non-termination-aware reward:* Task reward minus a penalty for
episodes that do not terminate successfully.

*Optional E4 --- Combined reward:* A combination of the strongest
individual conditions, only if time and compute permit.

A general shaped reward is:

#align(center)[
  $R_lambda = R_"task" - lambda dot C_"target"$
]

== Lambda variation

A small sweep is sufficient for the thesis. The exact numerical scale
depends on the reward magnitudes, but the experiment should include:

- $lambda = 0$: task-success-only baseline
- $lambda_"low"$: weak efficiency pressure
- $lambda_"medium"$: moderate efficiency pressure
- $lambda_"high"$: strong efficiency pressure

The coefficients should be selected so that the efficiency component
spans a meaningful range relative to the task reward. A short pilot can
determine concrete values. The key analysis plots task success,
targeted efficiency, and off-target behavior as functions of $lambda$.

== Experimental phases

*Phase 1 (in progress):* Consolidate the controlled pilot and the
difficulty calibration in the secondary environment, and settle the
environment-selection criteria above.

*Phase 2 (in progress):* Establish the base model and the
task-success-only policy in the primary environment, evaluated as a
paired comparison on the held-out and shifted splits.

*Phase 3:* Run the length-aware and non-termination-aware lambda sweeps
in the primary environment.

*Phase 4:* Repeat the baseline and the most informative reward
settings across at least two seeds.

*Optional Phase 5:* Repeat the non-termination condition on a second
base model of a different lineage, to check whether the outcome label
depends on the reference policy. The Llama 3.2 instruction-tuned family
is the candidate, because it is the comparable-size family whose chat
template already supports the native tool-calling interface the
pipeline uses. Gemma 3 was checked and does not, so adopting it would
mean writing a custom template and substituting a confound for a
control. The size-matched 1B variant is preferred over the 3B for
parity with the primary model, subject to an evaluation-only probe
confirming that it can perform the task at all. @sec:scope explains why
this arm is restricted to the non-termination condition.

= Interpretation of Outcomes

#table(

  table.header([*Outcome*], [*Interpretation*]),

  [Successful reward shaping],
  [The targeted inefficiency decreases, task success is preserved, and
   no relevant off-target deterioration is observed.],

  [Contaminated success],
  [The targeted inefficiency decreases and task success is preserved or
   improves, but an off-target behavior shifts alongside it. The gain
   can mask the shift, which makes this the outcome most easily
   misreported as a clean result.],

  [No meaningful effect],
  [The targeted behavior does not change substantially.],

  [Overcorrection],
  [The targeted inefficiency decreases, but task success deteriorates
   without a clear substitute behavior.],

  [Possible reward bias substitution],
  [The targeted inefficiency decreases, but another undesirable
   behavior increases or independent task quality declines alongside an
   off-target shift.],
)

Because the off-target panel is necessarily incomplete, the thesis
should state that no substitution was detected within the measured
indicators rather than claiming that a reward is universally safe
@lamparth2026substitution.

A campaign in which every reward condition lands in the no-effect row
remains a reportable result rather than a failed thesis. It locates the
point at which a shaped reward stops having any optimization footprint,
which is itself a constraint on where efficiency rewards are worth
applying.

// ---------- 8 Preliminary results ----------
= Preliminary Results <sec:results>

Both environment rejections reported in @sec:setup came out of the
pipeline work so far, so the choice of environment is a measured result
rather than a starting assumption.

Two task-success-only training runs have been evaluated so far, and
they do not yet point the same way. In the secondary environment,
task-success-only GRPO lifted task accuracy substantially above the
untrained base model at approximately six hours per expert on a single
L4 GPU, which is the order of magnitude the on-demand argument depends
on. That lift is provisional rather than quoted: the base-model probe
used fifty episodes and its confidence interval touches the trained
one, so a larger base evaluation is needed before the size of the gap
is claimed.

In the primary environment the same condition was compared against the
unmodified base model on identical instances of both evaluation splits.
On the held-out split the two policies do not differ significantly. On
the shifted split, task success falls from 0.870 to 0.790, at
$p = 0.021$ on a paired test over per-instance outcomes.

This is one condition in one environment, measured before any
efficiency reward has been applied, and it is reported for what it says
about the protocol rather than as a verdict on the method. An
evaluation restricted to the training distribution would have reported
that the training run changed nothing; only the shifted split makes the
out-of-distribution cost visible, which is the blind spot
@sec:protocol exists to cover. It is also a calibration signal about
the environment: a task set on which task-success-only training buys no
in-distribution headroom leaves a shaped reward little room to show an
effect in either direction, which is a reason to revisit the
environment under the criteria in @sec:setup rather than a finding
about reward design.

One earlier result is withdrawn rather than reported. A length-reward
sweep @chen2025diet in the secondary environment produced an apparent
null, but a defect in the token counting used by the shaping term
invalidates its measurements. The accuracy and cost figures above are
unaffected, because the task-success-only baseline carries no shaping
term.

// ---------- 9 Contribution and scope ----------
= Expected Contribution and Scope <sec:scope>

The thesis contributes an empirical transfer of reward bias
substitution from single-response reward shaping
@lamparth2026substitution to sequential LLM-agent trajectories. It
provides a controlled comparison of efficiency rewards and a practical
protocol for evaluating whether apparent efficiency improvements are
genuine or accompanied by off-target behavioral changes.

The scope is intentionally limited to one primary environment, one
small base model as primary reference policy, two reward interventions,
and a small lambda sweep. What is fixed is the research questions and
the evaluation protocol; the environment and the base model are the
instruments used to answer them. If calibration shows that a chosen
environment cannot express the contrast the questions require, it is
replaced under the criteria in @sec:setup rather than kept for
continuity. Those criteria are stated in advance so that such a change
is a documented selection step rather than an ad hoc one.

Two further boundaries inside that scope are drawn deliberately rather
than by omission, and both follow from the paper's own analysis.

Out-of-distribution evaluation stays inside the primary environment,
using withheld task families rather than a second environment. A
cross-environment split would confound a change of task family with the
effect of the reward, and would require a separate difficulty
calibration before the second environment's numbers could be compared
at all. Withheld families vary the task while holding the interface,
the action space, and the reward definition fixed, which is the
comparison the protocol needs.

The recommendation to validate against at least two reference policies
of different lineages @lamparth2026substitution cannot be followed
uniformly here, for a reason worth stating precisely. Among
instruction-tuned models of comparable size that expose a native
tool-calling chat template, the available alternative lineage does not
emit a separate reasoning trace. The length-aware reward targets
exactly that trace, so for that condition lineage cannot be varied
independently of the measured feature, and a second-lineage arm would
answer a different question: whether a length reward does anything when
there is no reasoning block to compress. The non-termination condition
does not have this problem, because failing to terminate is a property
of the episode rather than of a reasoning block, and both lineages can
express it. The second reference policy is therefore planned for that
condition, where it is a genuine control, and every other result is
reported as conditional on a single reference policy. Since the
impossibility construction varies only the reference policy while
holding every audit-side observable fixed @lamparth2026substitution,
that conditionality is a property of the claim rather than a caveat
that further compute would remove.

The thesis does not aim to provide a universal detector of all possible
off-target behavior or a formal safety guarantee.
