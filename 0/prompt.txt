// ---------- 1 Motivation ----------
= Motivation

Reasoning LLMs overthink. They produce chains of thought far longer
than the task requires, a behavior documented across models and
benchmarks @sui2025survey. In an agentic system the cost compounds:
the agent pays the reasoning bill at every step of a trajectory, and
work on agentic tasks shows that curbing overthinking can cut compute
and raise task success at the same time @cuadron2025agentic.

Deployed agents are usually specialists in practice. An agent sits in
front of one environment, say a shop system or a ticket queue, and
works the same class of tasks over and over. A large general model
treats every instance as a fresh reasoning problem and spends
accordingly. A small model fine-tuned into an expert for exactly this
task class can spend far less per solve. It can also solve more:
accuracy does not keep rising with longer reasoning but peaks at a
task-dependent optimum @wu2025length, so where the generalist's
effort is badly calibrated, a specialist trained toward that optimum
can beat it on success as well as cost.

Such specialization only helps if producing the expert is cheap. The
setting we target is close to online: a new task class arrives, a
short fine-tuning run turns a small base model into its expert, and
the expert takes over if it beats the model currently handling the
task. Training cost is therefore a constraint of the method itself,
and the thesis reports what an expert costs to produce next to what
it saves at inference.

We produce these experts with reinforcement learning, where what the
expert learns to spend is decided by the reward. Which reward signals
teach efficient behavior is open. A plain shorter-is-better penalty
overshoots the accuracy optimum and collapses reasoning
@wu2025length; our own pilot runs reproduce this collapse twice.
Entropy-based signals target the tokens where reasoning branches
@wang2025entropy but can inflate output, and pushing thinking further
can stop paying off entirely @ghosal2025mirage. On top of that, a
single mean length cannot tell whether a shorter model compressed
waste or skipped reasoning it needed, so overthinking and
underthinking have to be measured separately. Comparing these reward
signals under controlled conditions is the core of the thesis.

// ---------- 2 Research question and objective ----------
= Research question and objective

*Research question.* Which reward signals, alone or in combination,
let GRPO fine-tune a small LLM into a task expert that matches or
improves task success while spending fewer reasoning tokens, at a
fine-tuning cost low enough for on-demand specialization?

*Objective.* Answer this question empirically. We fine-tune a small
open model with GRPO @shao2024grpo in OpenEnv environments, one
reward configuration at a time, and measure every resulting expert on
four axes: task success, reasoning tokens spent, the split between
overthinking and underthinking, and the compute the fine-tuning
itself consumed. Conditions are compared across settings of varying
task difficulty and episode horizon. The result is a ranking of
reward signals, with the evidence to say which of them produce agents
that are cheaper and at least as good, and what such an expert costs
to train.

// ---------- 3 Approach ----------
= Approach

*Training.* GRPO @shao2024grpo is the training algorithm: critic-free,
with rewards normalized within a group of rollouts, so the training
loop stays small and fits the cheap-specialization constraint. The
base model is a small open LLM fine-tuned with LoRA adapters; the
pilot runs below use Qwen2-7B. The environment scores each episode,
and that score is the reward. There is no dataset answer key;
correctness comes from the environment itself.

*Environment: OpenEnv.* Training and evaluation run in OpenEnv
@openenv2025, the open environment-interface library started by
Meta's PyTorch team and Hugging Face. An OpenEnv environment is a
server with a Gym-style reset/step contract: the model acts through
tool calls, the environment executes them, returns observations, and
scores the episode. OpenEnv fits this thesis for two reasons. First,
one interface covers many tasks: classic agentic benchmarks such as
ALFWorld @shridhar2021alfworld and WebShop @yao2022webshop each come
as a single environment with its own harness, while OpenEnv puts a
growing catalog of environments behind one API. Second, that same
property is what the on-demand story needs: a new task is a new
environment behind an unchanged interface, so specializing for it
means pointing the same training loop at a different environment
server rather than building new infrastructure. The pipeline already
runs end-to-end against a single-turn OpenEnv environment with
procedurally generated tasks and a seed-disjoint train/test split;
multi-turn environments plug into the same interface and are the
first infrastructure step of the thesis.

*Reward signals.* Every condition contains the environment's
task-success score as the accuracy anchor. On top of it we study two
efficiency signals. The first is a correctness-coupled cosine length
reward: accuracy peaks at an interior, difficulty-dependent length
@wu2025length, and a reward shaped as in DIET @chen2025diet aims the
model at that optimum instead of at zero tokens, so it compresses
where it can and keeps reasoning where it must. The second is token
entropy: a minority of high-entropy tokens act as forking points
where reasoning branches @wang2025entropy, which is the exploration
an agentic task demands; whether rewarding it protects needed
exploration or inflates effort without return @ghosal2025mirage is
one of the things the comparison will show. Combinations of signals
are tested under two composition rules, plain addition and the
advantage weighting of DIET @chen2025diet. Composition is a tool
here, not a contribution.

*Evaluation.* Every condition reports task success, reasoning tokens
per episode, overthinking and underthinking rates against fixed
length thresholds, the correlation between task difficulty and spent
length (efficient compression is difficulty-aware; flat compression
is a warning sign), and the wall-clock and GPU cost of the
fine-tuning run. Each expert is also probed outside its training
distribution to check that specialization did not destroy general
capability.

*Experiment plan.*

+ E0: success-only baseline. Outcome-only GRPO already shortens
  reasoning on its own @wu2025length, so every shaped signal must
  beat this trained baseline, not the raw model.
+ E1: each efficiency signal alone, on top of task success.
+ E2: signal combinations, under both composition rules.
+ E3: the best conditions repeated across settings, on harder tasks
  and longer horizons.

// ---------- 4 Preliminary results ----------
= Preliminary results

Two pilot batches have run so far, on single-turn math tasks with
Qwen2-7B and a single seed each, so all of the following is
preliminary.

- The correctness-coupled cosine length reward matched the baseline's
  task accuracy (0.795 vs. 0.785, statistically tied) while cutting
  mean reasoning length by about 40%. It also kept the correlation
  between task difficulty and spent length close to baseline, so the
  compression is difficulty-aware rather than flat.
- A plain linear length penalty collapsed to about 17 output tokens
  and roughly a third of baseline accuracy, in both batches and under
  two different rollout backends. The shape of the length reward
  matters more than its weight.
- The entropy results flipped sign between the two batches. No
  conclusion until multi-seed replication.
- Plain signal addition beat advantage-weighted composition in the
  combined runs, which runs against the prior from DIET
  @chen2025diet. Also unconfirmed until replicated.

Since these batches the pipeline has moved from dataset-scored math
to its first OpenEnv environment: the same training loop now runs
against a live environment server whose score is the reward.
